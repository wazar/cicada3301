# O01 — fixed opcode arity does not predict held unit lengths

A new procedural representation was executed: the first rune of each explicit unit acts as an instruction opcode, and predicts the number of remaining operands through total unit length. Held suffix prediction gives mean gain **−0.0365129 nats/unit** over a page-specific length baseline, with full-procedure circular-shift tail **0.184** (999 nulls). There is no predictive candidate for this fixed shared-opcode family.

Four actual-size controls with independent fixed opcode-to-arity partitions each give positive gain1.8831–1.8848 and tail0.01 against99 full-procedure nulls each. A uniform-random-head fixture gives −0.0375402 and tail0.18; one fixture is descriptive, not a measured false-positive rate. Controls preserve exact page sizes and unit lengths but replace heads, so they do not match all real rune frequencies or head/body doublets. Detection power for strong deterministic arity is demonstrated; subtle or context-dependent grammar remains outside the guarantee.

The two mechanisms considered were fixed variable-length plaintext token expansion and opcode/operand parsing. Deterministic token encoding with resets at preserved word boundaries retains repeated-word equality, already strongly constrained by F09 in its source register. Context-sensitive expansion could defeat that test, but specifying arbitrary context without a new prediction would not make useful new work. O01 therefore tested the different procedural prediction. It does not test all variable-length token encoders, nor does a possible opcode/length association uniquely identify grammar rather than lexical token structure.

Novelty bounds: J03 expands source runes and evaluates homophone count capacity; F09 tests whole-unit repetition; F05 compares punctuation classes with endpoints/lengths; H06/07 test delimiter-category order. O01 instead learns the within-unit first-symbol/length relationship and measures continuation prediction. It uses neither ciphertext pairs, additive keys, English quadgrams, physical punctuation classifications, nor new source-language assumptions.

All45 discovery pages contain2,355 explicit units. Per page, firstfloor(2m/3) units train; final804 units test. A pooled29×8 Laplace-smoothed head-given-length likelihood is combined with each page's smoothed training length distribution. Classes are lengths1..7 and>=8. No alternative head position, binning or smoothing strength was selected. Nulls rotate each page's complete head sequence relative to fixed lengths and rerun the entire estimator. They preserve head counts and cyclic order, but assume phase exchangeability relative to lengths; they are not an all-language null. The suffixes are unused in this experiment's fitting, not pristine global holdouts.

Exactly1,500 complete fits ran in0.961 logged seconds:400 control fits,1,000 real/null fits,100 baseline-fixture fits. `O01-evidence.json.gz` retains all units/runes/source positions, head and length maps, partitions, predictions, trained counts, complete null scores and all rotations. Rune reconstruction exactly recovers every input stream. The statistic ignores body rune identities, physical layout, and delimiter types; those omissions are explicit, and original mapped units remain intact.
